\chapter{Nanostructures}

When the dimensions of a solid are reduced to the nanometer scale---comparable to the electron wavelength or the mean free path---quantum confinement and surface effects dominate. This chapter presents experimental data on one-dimensional (1D) and zero-dimensional (0D) systems.

\section{Imaging Techniques for Nanostructures}

The scanning tunneling microscope (Chapter~1) and the atomic force microscope are the primary tools for imaging nanostructures with atomic resolution.

\subsection{Atomic Manipulation with the STM}

Don Eigler at IBM Almaden demonstrated that the STM can not only image individual atoms but also \textit{move} them~\cite{eigler1991switch}. In 1990, Eigler and Schweizer positioned 35 xenon atoms on a Ni(110) surface to spell ``IBM''---the first demonstration of atomic-scale engineering.

Eigler et al.\ (1991) further demonstrated an \textbf{atomic switch}: a single Xe atom could be transferred reversibly between the STM tip and the Ni surface by voltage pulses. This paper in \textit{Nature} (1132 citations) opened the field of single-atom manipulation.


\section{Conductance Quantization}

In a quantum point contact---a narrow constriction between two electron reservoirs---the electrical conductance is quantized in units of the \textbf{conductance quantum}:
\begin{equation}
  G_0 = \frac{2e^2}{h} = \SI{7.748e-5}{\siemens} = (12906\;\Omega)^{-1}
\end{equation}

Each transverse mode that fits through the constriction contributes one quantum of conductance. As the constriction is widened (by gate voltage), the conductance increases in steps of $G_0$---the famous ``conductance staircase.''

This was first observed independently by van Wees et al.\ (1988) in a GaAs point contact and by Wharam et al.\ (1988) in a split-gate device.


\section{Quantum Dots: Artificial Atoms}

A quantum dot confines electrons in all three dimensions, producing discrete energy levels like an atom. The \textbf{Coulomb blockade} occurs when the charging energy $E_C = e^2/2C$ (where $C$ is the dot capacitance) exceeds $k_BT$: adding a single electron to the dot requires a measurable voltage step:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta V = \frac{e}{C}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Semiconductor Nanocrystals}

Colloidal semiconductor nanocrystals (CdSe, InP) exhibit size-tunable photoluminescence: as the dot diameter decreases from \SI{6}{\nano\metre} to \SI{2}{\nano\metre}, the emission shifts from red to blue due to quantum confinement. This work, pioneered by Brus and Alivisatos, was recognized with the 2023 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
